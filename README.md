# E-Commerce Order Analysis in Excel

## Overview

What can 1,200 e-commerce orders tell us about a business?

This project explores that question using Microsoft Excel. I analysed e-commerce order records to understand business performance, product contributions, customer payment preferences, referral sources, and order delivery.

Rather than focusing only on charts, I wanted to understand what the numbers revealed and how the findings could inform business decisions.

The project includes data exploration, PivotTables, charts, KPI calculations, and an interactive dashboard.

## Business Questions

The analysis focused on six key questions:

1. **Business Performance:** How has recorded order value changed over time?
2. **Monthly Trends:** Which months recorded higher or lower order values?
3. **Product Performance:** Which products contributed the most recorded order value?
4. **Payment Methods:** How were customers paying for their orders?
5. **Referral Sources:** Which referral sources were associated with the highest number of orders?
6. **Order Delivery:** What proportion of recorded orders was marked as delivered?

## Tools Used

- **Microsoft Excel** — Data exploration, calculations, and analysis.
- **PivotTables** — Summarising and comparing order data across different categories.
- **Charts** — Communicating trends and comparisons.
- **Excel Dashboard** — Bringing key metrics and visualisations together.
- **Slicers** — Allowing users to interact with the dashboard and explore the data.

## Key Findings

### 1. Business Performance Over Time

The annual view suggested a decline in recorded order value. However, the 2025 dataset covered only January to June, making a direct comparison with complete previous years potentially misleading.

**Business implication:** Comparing equivalent periods across years would provide a fairer assessment of performance.

### 2. Monthly Trends

The aggregated monthly view showed a peak around June, followed by a sharp drop in July.

Because the chart grouped the same months across different years, the pattern does not establish recurring seasonality.

**Business implication:** Reviewing monthly performance year by year could help the business understand changes in demand and plan inventory and promotions more effectively.

### 3. Product Performance

Chairs, Printers, Laptops, and Tablets were among the stronger contributors to recorded order value, while Phones recorded a lower order value.

**Business implication:** Product decisions should consider customer demand, inventory levels, and profit margins alongside recorded order value.

### 4. Payment Methods

Online payments recorded the highest number of orders at 258, followed by Cash at 246.

The other payment methods recorded relatively similar order counts.

**Business implication:** Comparing order outcomes across payment methods could help the business identify potential checkout or payment-related issues.

### 5. Referral Sources

Instagram was associated with the highest number of orders, at 259, followed by Email at 250 and Google at 241.

These figures represent order counts, not marketing profitability or return on investment.

**Business implication:** Comparing referral sources by completed orders, order value, and marketing costs could help the business make better marketing decisions.

### 6. Order Delivery

The dashboard displayed an order delivery rate of 19.25%, calculated as the proportion of all recorded orders marked as Delivered.

**Business implication:** Reviewing the distribution of order statuses could help the business identify outstanding orders and investigate delivery or fulfilment challenges.

## Dashboard Features

The Excel dashboard brings the analysis together in one place, featuring:

- Key performance indicators.
- Annual and monthly order value trends.
- Recorded order value by product.
- Order counts by payment method.
- Order counts by referral source.
- Interactive slicers for exploring the data.
